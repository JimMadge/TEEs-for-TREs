Trusted Execution Environments for TREs

When to use them and what you need to know

Trusted Execution Environments

TEEs encrypt data in use.
With a TEE, you can use a computer in a way that is private from other users, even the owner of that computer.

Trusted Execution Environments

Attestation

A TEE is able to prove it is secure and configured correctly.

The secure processor collects and signs evidence.

Generally, the evidence is assessed by a verifier, who collects endorsements from the CPU vendor. They produce a report, used to decide whether or not to trust the TEE.

Trusted Execution Environments

What a TEE protects against

  • Attack from host/hypervisor
  • Vulnerabilities on the host
  • Some types of physical attack

Trusted Execution Environments

What a TEE doesn't protect against

  • Software and processes inside the TEE
  • Users with TEE access and social engineering attacks
  • Some side-channel attacks

When Not to Add More

The cost may outweigh the benefit
  • Effective non-technical controls
  • Overlaps with existing controls
  • Complex implementation of migration
  • Unsuitable, overly restrictive controls

Recommendations

Use a TEE as part of your TRE when,

  1. When strong isolation from the host is required
  2. Using low trust devices
  3. When high sensitive data must be used

Recommendations

Strong Isolation

There are cases where extra protection from compromised hosts is valuable.

TRE infrastructure is not exclusive

Systems used for confidential and non-confidential work have higher risk.

Low-Trust Devices

TEEs can enable new models of trusted research.

  • Mesh/distributed TREs
  • Data subject devices

Extreme Data Sensitivity

When it is not possible to modify data to reduce disclosure risk (as in Five Safes).

Situations where highly-motivated, sophisticated attackers may target the TRE infrastructure or infrastructure operator staff.

In the future, LLM agents may significantly lower the barrier to launching this kind of attack.

Considerations

TEEs are not a "drop-in" solution.

Considerations

Hardware Support and Availability

Support is currently limited to the latest data centre hardware

Considerations

TEE and Host Configuration

  • BIOS (UEFI)
  • Compatible OS and kernel
  • Guest OS (for confidential VMs)
  • Trusted devices such as GPUs

Considerations

TRE Design and Implementation Cost

TREs will need to be modified.

Software inside a TEE must be trusted. Care must be taken to minimise the trusted application(s).

Considerations

Attestation Infrastructure

If you don't inspect an attestation report, you don't know if a TEE is secure!

Attestation policies must be developed and maintained.

Some approaches will require more infrastructure, for example workload-level attestation, or ejecting non-compliant TEEs.

Considerations

Vendor Lock-in and Portability

Adopting TEEs may require building on vendor-specific features, APIs and attestation fields.

Generic interfaces to TEEs are in the early stages of development
